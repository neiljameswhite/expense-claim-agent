# Expense Policy
**Version 1.0** · Effective 1 January 2026

*This policy is a fixture written for the Expense Claim Agent proof of concept. In a real deployment it would be an existing organisational document supplied as an input.*

---

## 1. Scope and principles

**1.1** This policy applies to all employees claiming reimbursement for expenses incurred in the course of business.

**1.2** Expenses must be incurred wholly, exclusively and necessarily for business purposes.

**1.3** All claims must be supported by a valid receipt. A valid receipt shows the retailer, the date, a description of what was purchased, the individual item costs and the total paid.

**1.4** The amount claimed must equal the total shown on the receipt. Partial claims against a larger receipt are not permitted.

---

## 2. Categories and limits

**2.1** Every claim must be assigned to one of the categories below. The category must reflect the nature of the expense actually incurred, as evidenced by the receipt.

| Category | Limit | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Subsistence | £40 | Per day |
| Travel | £150 | Per journey |
| Accommodation | £180 | Per night |
| Client entertainment | £75 | Per head |
| Office supplies | £100 | Per claim |
| Other | £50 | Per claim |

**2.2** A claim at or below the stated limit requires no further justification. A claim exceeding the limit is permitted only where clause 4 is satisfied.

**2.3** Where a receipt evidences an expense falling within a listed category, that category must be selected. "Other" must not be used to avoid a category limit.

---

## 3. The "Other" category

**3.1** "Other" is reserved for legitimate business expenses that do not fall within any listed category.

**3.2** A claim under "Other" must include a written rationale describing what the expense was and why it was necessary for business.

**3.3** A rationale is supported where it describes an expense that is:
- clearly business-related, and
- not within the scope of any listed category, and
- specific about what was purchased and for what purpose.

**3.4** A rationale is not supported where it:
- describes an expense falling within a listed category, or
- asserts business necessity without describing the expense, or
- describes a personal expense.

**3.5** Examples of expenses appropriate to "Other": conference registration fees, professional body subscriptions, visa and immigration fees, courier charges for business documents.

---

## 4. Exceeding a category limit

**4.1** A claim may exceed its category limit only where a written rationale is provided and that rationale is supported by this clause.

**4.2** A rationale is supported where the excess arose from one of the following, and the rationale states which:

- **(a) Prior written approval.** Approval was obtained before the expense was incurred, and the rationale references the approver and the date.
- **(b) Travel disruption.** Cancellation, delay or diversion outside the claimant's control required additional or replacement arrangements.
- **(c) Scarcity of alternatives.** No comparable alternative was reasonably available at or below the limit at the time of booking.
- **(d) Client requirement.** The client specified the venue, arrangement or standard, and declining was not reasonably practicable.

**4.3** A rationale is not supported where it:
- asserts necessity or reasonableness without stating which of the grounds in 4.2 applies, or
- describes a matter of personal preference or convenience, or
- relies on a ground that the stated facts do not establish.

**4.4** Clause 4.2(c) requires a judgement as to what was reasonably available. Where the rationale asserts scarcity but provides no supporting detail, the claim should be treated as requiring human assessment rather than approved or declined on the rationale alone.

**4.5** There is no upper ceiling above which clause 4 ceases to apply. A well-founded rationale supports the excess regardless of amount.

---

## 5. VAT

**5.1** Where a receipt shows a VAT registration number and the expense falls within a category listed in 5.2, the claimant must record the VAT amount shown on the receipt.

**5.2** VAT must be recorded for: **Subsistence**, **Accommodation**, **Office supplies**.

**5.3** VAT need not be recorded for: **Travel**, **Client entertainment**, **Other**. Travel is frequently zero-rated and client entertainment is not recoverable.

**5.4** Where a receipt carries no VAT registration number, no VAT amount is required regardless of category.

**5.5** The VAT amount recorded must match the amount shown on the receipt.

---

## 6. Claims that cannot be assessed

**6.1** Where the supporting document is not a receipt, or where any of the fields required by 1.3 cannot be read, the claim cannot be verified against its evidence.

**6.2** Such a claim is assessed against the values declared by the claimant, and the inability to verify is recorded as a finding in its own right.

---

## 7. Categories in detail

**7.1 Subsistence.** Meals and non-alcoholic refreshments purchased while working away from the normal place of work. The limit applies per day, not per meal.

**7.2 Travel.** Rail, air, taxi, fuel, parking and tolls incurred on business journeys. Ordinary commuting between home and the normal place of work is not claimable.

**7.3 Accommodation.** Room rate and directly associated charges for overnight stays on business. Meals charged to a room are claimed as subsistence, not accommodation.

**7.4 Client entertainment.** Hospitality provided to clients or prospective clients. The rationale must identify the client. The limit applies per attendee, including the employee.

**7.5 Office supplies.** Consumables and low-value equipment purchased for business use where procurement through normal channels was not practicable.

---

*End of policy.*
