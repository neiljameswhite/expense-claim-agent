# Expense Policy
**Version 2.1** · Effective 1 January 2026

*This policy is a fixture written for the Expense Claim Agent prototype. In a real deployment it would be an existing organisational document supplied as an input.*

*Supersedes version 1.0. Changes: VAT recording now turns on what the receipt evidences rather than the claim category; subsistence requires the employee to have been working away from their normal place of work, at one of two limits; mileage separated from travel, with its own evidence requirements and no per-claim limit.*

---

## 1. Scope and principles

**1.1** This policy applies to all employees claiming reimbursement for expenses incurred in the course of business.

**1.2** Expenses must be incurred wholly, exclusively and necessarily for business purposes.

**1.3** All claims must be supported by a valid receipt. A valid receipt shows the retailer, the date, a description of what was purchased, the individual item costs and the total paid.

**1.4** The amount claimed must equal the total shown on the receipt, except for mileage claims, which are governed by clause 6.

---

## 2. Categories and limits

**2.1** Every claim must be assigned to one of the categories below. The category must reflect the nature of the expense actually incurred, as evidenced by the receipt.

| Category | Limit | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Subsistence | £40 or £15 | Per day, see clause 5 |
| Travel | £150 | Per journey |
| Mileage | £0.45 | Per mile, no per-claim limit, see clause 6 |
| Accommodation | £180 | Per night |
| Client entertainment | £75 | Per head |
| Office supplies | £100 | Per claim |
| Other | £50 | Per claim |

**2.2** A claim at or below the applicable limit requires no further justification. A claim exceeding the limit is permitted only where clause 4 is satisfied.

**2.3** Where a receipt evidences an expense falling within a listed category, that category must be selected. "Other" must not be used to avoid a category limit.

---

## 3. The "Other" category

**3.1** "Other" is reserved for legitimate business expenses that do not fall within any listed category.

**3.2** A claim under "Other" must include a written description of what the expense was and why it was necessary for business.

**3.3** A description is supported where it identifies an expense that is:
- clearly business-related, and
- not within the scope of any listed category, and
- specific about what was purchased and for what purpose.

**3.4** A description is not supported where it:
- identifies an expense falling within a listed category, or
- asserts business necessity without describing the expense, or
- describes a personal expense.

**3.5** Examples of expenses appropriate to "Other": conference registration fees, professional body subscriptions, visa and immigration fees, courier charges for business documents.

---

## 4. Exceeding a category limit

**4.1** A claim may exceed its category limit only where a written explanation is provided and that explanation is supported by this clause.

**4.2** An explanation is supported where the excess arose from one of the following, and the explanation states which:

- **(a) Prior written approval.** Approval was obtained before the expense was incurred, and the explanation references the approver and the date.
- **(b) Travel disruption.** Cancellation, delay or diversion outside the claimant's control required additional or replacement arrangements.
- **(c) Scarcity of alternatives.** No comparable alternative was reasonably available at or below the limit at the time of booking.
- **(d) Client requirement.** The client specified the venue, arrangement or standard, and declining was not reasonably practicable.

**4.3** An explanation is not supported where it:
- asserts necessity or reasonableness without stating which of the grounds in 4.2 applies, or
- describes a matter of personal preference or convenience, or
- relies on a ground that the stated facts do not establish.

**4.4** Clause 4.2(c) requires a judgement as to what was reasonably available. Where the explanation asserts scarcity but provides no supporting detail, the claim cannot be determined on the explanation alone.

**4.5** There is no upper ceiling above which clause 4 ceases to apply. A well-founded explanation supports the excess regardless of amount.

---

## 5. Subsistence

**5.1** Subsistence may be claimed only where the employee was working away from their normal place of work. It is not claimable for meals taken on an ordinary working day at the employee's usual site.

**5.2** Two limits apply:

- **£40 per day** where the employee stayed away from home overnight in order to work at a client or company site.
- **£15 per day** where the employee travelled to a client site and back on the same day, and was away from home for more than twelve hours.

**5.3** Every subsistence claim must include a description establishing which limit applies. The description must state:

- for an overnight claim, that the employee stayed away from home overnight, and the site or client visited;
- for a day claim, that the employee was away from home for more than twelve hours, and the name of the client visited.

**5.4** Receipts are required for subsistence under both limits. Neither limit is a flat allowance.

---

## 6. Mileage

**6.1** Mileage may be claimed for business journeys made in the employee's own vehicle. The amount claimed is the distance travelled multiplied by the rate stated in 2.1.

**6.2** A mileage claim must be supported by a fuel receipt. The amount claimed is not expected to equal the receipt total. The receipt evidences a fuel purchase; the journey itself is established by the description required at 6.4.

**6.3** The fuel receipt must be dated on or before the date of the journey, and no more than fourteen days before it. A receipt dated after the journey does not support it.

**6.4** A mileage claim must include a description of the journey stating:

- the name of the client or site visited,
- its location, and
- the UK postcode of both the origin and the destination.

**6.5** Where any of the elements in 6.4 is absent, the journey is not established and the claim is not payable.

**6.6** Ordinary commuting between home and the employee's normal place of work is not claimable.

**6.7** No per-claim limit applies to mileage. The amount is determined by the distance travelled, and clause 4 does not apply.

---

## 7. VAT

**7.1** Where a receipt shows a VAT registration number and the expense is prepared food or drink consumed on the premises, the claimant must record the VAT amount shown on the receipt.

**7.2** VAT need not be recorded for any other expense, including food or drink purchased as groceries or pre-packaged items for later consumption.

**7.3** Clause 7.1 applies to what the receipt evidences, not to the category selected. A restaurant meal claimed as client entertainment requires the VAT to be recorded in the same way as one claimed as subsistence.

**7.4** Where a receipt carries no VAT registration number, no VAT amount is required.

**7.5** The VAT amount recorded must match the amount shown on the receipt.

---

## 8. Claims that cannot be assessed

**8.1** Where the supporting document is not a receipt, or where any of the fields required by 1.3 cannot be read, the claim cannot be verified against its evidence.

**8.2** Such a claim is assessed against the values declared by the claimant, and the inability to verify is recorded as a finding in its own right.

---

## 9. Categories in detail

**9.1 Subsistence.** Meals and non-alcoholic refreshments purchased while working away from the normal place of work, subject to clause 5. The limit applies per day, not per meal.

**9.2 Travel.** Rail, air, taxi, parking and tolls incurred on business journeys. Fuel for the employee's own vehicle is claimed as mileage under clause 6, not as travel.

**9.3 Mileage.** Business journeys in the employee's own vehicle, subject to clause 6.

**9.4 Accommodation.** Room rate and directly associated charges for overnight stays on business. Meals charged to a room are claimed as subsistence, not accommodation.

**9.5 Client entertainment.** Hospitality provided to clients or prospective clients. The description must identify the client. The limit applies per attendee, including the employee.

**9.6 Office supplies.** Consumables and low-value equipment purchased for business use where procurement through normal channels was not practicable.

---

*End of policy.*
